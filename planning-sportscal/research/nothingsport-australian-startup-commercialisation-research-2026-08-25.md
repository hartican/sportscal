# NothingSport Australian startup and commercialisation research

**Checked:** 25 August 2026 (Australia/Sydney)  
**Scope:** A low-upfront-cash pathway for forming an Australian NothingSport company, bringing Jim into governance and product leadership, protecting IP, and testing advertising and referral revenue.

This is commercial research, not legal, tax, employment or patent advice. The recommended structure assumes an Australian-resident founder, an early-stage proprietary company and no external investors yet. A startup lawyer and accountant should approve the equity, employment and IP documents before Jim is appointed or securities are issued.

## Recommended path in one page

1. Form **NothingSport Pty Ltd**, a proprietary company limited by shares. Register it directly through the Australian Government's [Business Registration Service](https://register.business.gov.au/registration/type?type=Company), use the statutory replaceable rules initially, and keep the founder as the sole shareholder/director for the few weeks needed to settle Jim's package.
2. Apply for the company ABN/TFN at the same time. Register for GST only when turnover requires it or there is a clear voluntary-registration benefit. If the public-facing brand omits “Pty Ltd”, register **NothingSport** as a business name too.
3. Do not call Jim a non-executive director while he is Product Lead. A Product Lead participating in day-to-day management is functionally an **executive director**. Alternatively, make him Product Lead plus a board adviser/observer now, then appoint him as a director when the working and equity arrangements are settled.
4. Do not leave an ongoing Product Lead role “unpaid” on the assumption that equity cures it. Fair Work status turns on the real relationship. Put the role, minimum cash remuneration, hours/scope, equity and IP assignment in writing.
5. A reasonable **negotiating proposal**, not a market valuation, is 0.5% ordinary equity after tax valuation plus options up to another 2.5% on a fully diluted basis, vesting over four years with a one-year cliff and monthly vesting thereafter. Keep board appointment, employment and equity as separate legal arrangements.
6. Assign the founder's and contributors' existing code, designs, data structures, domains and goodwill to the company. Then file the **NothingSport word mark** in only the core classes justified by the near-term business. Defer patents unless a patent attorney identifies a genuinely technical invention and reviews prior public disclosures.
7. Monetise first with contextual, clearly labelled, directly sold Feed cards and the confirmed Australian [Ticketmaster affiliate program](https://developer.ticketmaster.com/partners/distribution-partners/affiliate-sign-up/). Ordinary links to NRL, AFL and broadcasters do not generate revenue without a contract.
8. Use plain-text official watch links and independently sourced/licensed facts for the MVP. Official NRL/AFL data, images, logos and marks require a commercial licence or express permission; do not scrape them.

## 1. Formation and minimum government cash cost

The Australian Government identifies a proprietary limited company as the common structure for a small business that needs a legal entity separate from its owner. ASIC requires at least one director who ordinarily lives in Australia; a company secretary is optional for a proprietary company. See [register a company](https://business.gov.au/registrations/register-a-company), [ASIC's registration steps](https://asic.gov.au/for-business/registering-a-company/steps-to-register-a-company/) and [minimum officeholders](https://asic.gov.au/for-business/registering-a-company/steps-to-register-a-company/minimum-officeholders/).

| Item | Current government fee | Lean decision |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Register an Australian proprietary company | **$636** | Required |
| ABN, TFN, GST and PAYG registrations | **$0** through the government service | ABN/TFN now; PAYG when needed; GST as below |
| Business name, one year | **$47** | Likely needed if trading publicly as “NothingSport” without “Pty Ltd” |
| Business name, three years | **$108** | Only $33 cheaper than three annual renewals; one year preserves flexibility |
| ASIC annual review fee for a proprietary company | **$342 a year** | Budget from year two; this is not optional |
| Director ID | **$0** | Founder and Jim each apply personally before appointment |

The current ASIC and business-name fees took effect on 1 July 2026: [Australian Government fee update](https://business.gov.au/news/changes-for-businesses-from-1-july-2026). ASIC fees do not include the variable professional cost of a constitution, shareholder agreement, tax advice, insurance or bookkeeping.

ASIC explains that a company name and a business name do different jobs: if the legal entity is **NothingSport Pty Ltd** but trades simply as **NothingSport**, the trading name should also be registered. Neither registration gives trade mark protection: [ASIC name distinction](https://asic.gov.au/for-business/registering-a-business-name/before-you-register-a-business-name/trading-names/the-difference-between-a-trading-name-business-name-and-company-name/).

### Lean incorporation sequence

- Confirm availability of the company/business name and run a separate trade mark clearance search. Availability in one register does not establish rights in the other.
- The founder obtains a [director ID](https://www.abrs.gov.au/director-identification-number/about-director-id), records written consent to act, chooses the registered office and incorporates with ordinary shares and the statutory replaceable rules. A director ID is free, unique for life and must be obtained before appointment; an individual can apply up to 12 months in advance: [ABRS timing](https://www.abrs.gov.au/director-identification-number/who-needs-apply-and-when).
- Apply for ABN and tax registrations through the [Business Registration Service](https://register.business.gov.au/registration/type?type=Company), then open a company bank account and keep company and personal expenditure separate.
- Maintain the member/share register, consents and resolutions. ASIC must be notified of a later share issue within 28 days; a timely Form 484 update is free: [ASIC share issues](https://www.asic.gov.au/for-business-and-companies/companies/company-share-and-shareholder-rules-and-changes/company-share-issues).
- Each year, check the ASIC annual statement, pass the solvency resolution and pay the annual review fee: [ASIC annual statements](https://asic.gov.au/for-business/running-a-company/annual-statements/).

GST registration becomes compulsory when current or projected GST turnover reaches **$75,000**; the ATO says to register within 21 days of becoming required. Registration below the threshold is optional, normally for at least 12 months, but adds BAS administration: [ATO GST registration](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/registering-for-gst). An accountant should also confirm whether the company qualifies for the 25% base-rate-entity company tax rate rather than 30%: [ATO company tax rates](https://www.ato.gov.au/tax-rates-and-codes/company-tax-rates).

## 2. Jim: board role, Product Lead and equity

### Non-executive versus executive

“Non-executive director” is a description of how a director works, not a lighter statutory office. ASIC and sections 180–183 of the Corporations Act impose the same baseline duties on all directors: care and diligence, good faith and proper purpose, no improper use of position or information, oversight of financial records and preventing insolvent trading. See [ASIC officeholder duties](https://www.asic.gov.au/for-business/running-a-company/company-officeholder-duties/) and the [current Corporations Act compilation](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00818/latest/text).

| Arrangement | Benefit | Cost/risk | When it fits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Product Lead + board adviser/observer** | Jim contributes to meetings and product without statutory director exposure; easiest to change | No board vote or formal director status | Best lean interim arrangement while role, pay and equity are documented |
| **Product Lead + director** | Proper board vote and strong alignment | He is functionally an **executive director**; full director duties, conflicts around his pay/performance, and a two-person board can deadlock | Appropriate if both parties genuinely want shared governance now |
| **Non-executive director only** | Independent challenge and governance focus | Jim should not also run day-to-day product; may justify a board fee/equity separate from operating work | Appropriate later if another person becomes Product Lead |

If Jim gets both requested roles, describe him accurately as **Director and Product Lead** or **Executive Director, Product**. Put the product employment/contract, board appointment and equity grant in separate documents so ending one does not accidentally determine the others. Require conflicts to be declared and Jim to abstain where appropriate on his own compensation or performance. A shareholder agreement must also solve a 1–1 board deadlock.

### Unpaid work is the immediate risk

Fair Work says an unpaid arrangement may be lawful only where there is no employment relationship. Productive work that is expected, ongoing, integral to the business and primarily benefits the business points toward employment. A genuine Product Lead role has most of those indicators. Equity does not automatically replace award wages, minimum wages, leave or superannuation: [Fair Work unpaid work](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/starting-employment/unpaid-work). The National Minimum Wage is **$26.44 per hour / $1,004.90 per week from 1 July 2026**, but an applicable award may require more: [Fair Work minimum wages](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/pay-and-wages/minimum-wages).

Therefore:

- document the past advice separately and do not retrospectively call it employment without advice;
- define the prospective Product Lead's duties, authority, hours, cash pay, expenses and review point;
- get an employment lawyer/accountant to classify the role; and
- register PAYG withholding and pay compulsory super where required. The ATO specifically includes director fees in PAYG/STP and super obligations: [ATO payments to company directors](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/hiring-and-paying-your-workers/payg-withholding/payments-you-need-to-withhold-from/payments-to-company-directors).

### A small equity proposal

The following is a discussion anchor, not a claim about market-standard compensation:

- **0.5% fully diluted ordinary equity**, issued only after the accountant values and models the tax treatment, recognises Jim's contribution and gives genuine ownership now.
- **Up to 2.5% fully diluted options** for the Product Lead work, vesting over four years with a 12-month cliff and monthly vesting thereafter. Vesting stops on departure; carefully drafted good/bad-leaver rules deal with vested securities.
- No automatic equity merely for occupying the board seat. If the role later becomes genuinely non-executive, agree a separate board fee or board grant.
- No single-trigger windfall on a sale. If acceleration is negotiated, prefer limited double-trigger acceleration: a change of control plus Jim losing the role.

Direct shares make Jim a shareholder immediately but are difficult to recover and can create an immediate tax issue. Options delay ownership until exercise and support vesting, but require plan rules, a defensible exercise price, disclosures and annual reporting. Issuing equity for past services can also have tax consequences; do not use a nominal value merely because the company is pre-revenue.

The ATO's startup ESS concession can defer the ordinary tax burden to CGT treatment for qualifying discounted shares/options, but only if all conditions are met. Key conditions include an unlisted group under 10 years old, aggregate turnover no more than $50 million, Australian-resident employer, minimum holding period, price/discount rules and the participant ownership/voting limit. See [ATO ESS startup concession and key changes](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/corporate-tax-measures-and-assurance/employee-share-schemes/in-detail/key-ess-changes-in-detail). ESS statements are generally due to participants by 14 July and the annual report to the ATO by 14 August: [ATO ESS reporting](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/corporate-tax-measures-and-assurance/employee-share-schemes/reporting-requirements-for-employee-share-schemes). Eligibility depends on Jim's exact employee/director/contractor relationship; an accountant should confirm it before grant.

Before issuing anything, a startup lawyer should prepare or review:

- shareholder agreement and, if necessary, a short tailored constitution;
- cap table expressed on an issued and fully diluted basis;
- board appointment/consent and the Product Lead employment or services agreement;
- option plan and grant terms, vesting, exercise, leaver and change-of-control rules;
- pre-emption, transfer, tag/drag, reserved matters, deadlock and information rights;
- IP assignment, confidentiality and moral-rights consents; and
- the decision path if the Product Lead role or board relationship ends.

## 3. Intellectual property: own it first, then register selectively

### Chain of title

IP Australia states that an employee's work created in relation to the employer's business will generally belong to the employer, but a contractor normally owns what they create unless a contract says otherwise: [who owns IP](https://www.ipaustralia.gov.au/understanding-ip/who-owns-ip). NothingSport should not assume that incorporation automatically transfers the pre-company product.

Execute written assignments from the founder and any contributor, including Jim where relevant, covering source code, object code, product specifications, designs, databases/selection and arrangement, editorial systems, domains, brand assets, documentation and associated goodwill. Include future-work assignment, confidentiality and moral-rights consent. Keep a register of open-source packages, third-party data and externally sourced marks/images, because an assignment cannot transfer rights the contributor never owned.

Australian copyright arises automatically for software code and protects expression, not the underlying product idea or method: [IP Australia computer-related inventions and copyright](https://www.ipaustralia.gov.au/patents/what-are-patents/what-computer-related-inventions-can-be-patented).

### Trade marks

Search the [Australian Trade Mark Search](https://search.ipaustralia.gov.au/trademarks) for exact, similar-sounding and logo marks before committing to the company/business name. No clearance opinion was completed for this research.

| Filing route | Current government fee | Use |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Standard application using the picklist | **$250 per class** | Cheapest direct filing, but no pre-filing examiner feedback |
| Standard application without the picklist | **$400 per class** | Only where the picklist cannot describe the service |
| TM Headstart | **$330 per class**: $200 request + $130 conversion | More expensive but provides an early assessment before formal filing |

Sources: [trade mark price calculator](https://www.ipaustralia.gov.au/trade-marks/timeframes-and-fees/trade-mark-price-calculator) and [TM Headstart](https://www.ipaustralia.gov.au/trade-marks/how-to-apply-for-a-trade-mark/pre-application-service-tm-headstart). A registered mark lasts 10 years and IP Australia says registration takes at least seven months: [what are trade marks](https://ipaustralia.gov.au/trade-marks/what-are-trade-marks).

Potential classes to review with an attorney are class 9 (downloadable software), 42 (SaaS/software services), 41 (sports information/entertainment) and 35 (advertising). Do not file all four reflexively. File the word mark first in the two or three classes supported by the actual 12–18 month plan; a two-class standard picklist filing is **$500**, while Headstart is **$660**.

### Patents

A provisional patent application costs **$100** in government fees but provides no enforceable protection and expires after 12 months unless followed by a complete application. IP Australia estimates professional help commonly costs **$4,000–$7,000** for a provisional: [provisional applications](https://www.ipaustralia.gov.au/patents/how-to-apply-for-a-standard-patent/provisional-patent-applications). A standard application starts at **$400**, before search, examination, attorney and renewal costs: [patent timeframes and fees](https://ipaustralia.gov.au/patents/timeframes-and-fees).

An app, commercial scheme or business method does not become patentable merely because it runs on a computer. IP Australia's current examination manual asks whether the substance is a technical contribution, such as solving a technical problem or improving computer operation: [computer-implemented inventions](https://manuals.ipaustralia.gov.au/patent/5.6.8.6-computer-implemented-inventions-schemes-and-business-methods). It must also be new, inventive, useful and adequately described.

NothingSport appears better protected initially through copyright, trade marks, contracts and secrecy around genuinely proprietary techniques. Because the product has already been exposed publicly, record every disclosure date and obtain patent-attorney advice before any patent-shaped feature is disclosed further. Australia's grace period may not preserve rights overseas. Do not spend even the $100 provisional fee without a prior-art and patentability discussion.

## 4. Native Feed advertising, privacy and consumer rules

### Lowest-complexity ad design

Start with **contextual** inventory selected from the card being viewed—sport, competition, city or event—not a behavioural profile assembled across sessions or sites. Sell a small number of placements directly, approve every creative, and measure aggregate impressions and clicks without third-party pixels. This is materially simpler than plugging an ad network into user data.

A launch policy could be:

- no ad among the first few cards, no more than one per 12–20 organic cards and a frequency cap;
- persistent **Advertisement** or **Paid promotion** label at the top of the card;
- for paid links, the adjacent disclosure **NothingSport may earn a commission if you buy**;
- sponsor identity and destination domain visible before click;
- ads never silently alter editorial rankings or “must watch” decisions;
- no gambling, tobacco/vapes, adult, political, high-risk financial/crypto or unsubstantiated health products at launch; and
- an advertiser insertion order covering substantiation, IP rights, prohibited content, takedown, privacy, measurement, payment, indemnity and cancellation.

The ACL prohibits false or misleading claims and omissions: [ACCC guidance](https://www.accc.gov.au/business/advertising-and-promotions/false-or-misleading-claims). The advertising industry's AANA Code also requires advertising to be clearly distinguishable and says vague or unexplained labels may be inadequate: [AANA Code of Ethics](https://aana.com.au/code-of-ethics/). A Feed-like visual treatment is acceptable only if the commercial nature is immediately obvious.

### Privacy and tracking

Many businesses with annual turnover of $3 million or less are currently exempt from the Privacy Act, but important exceptions apply—including some businesses that trade in personal information. The OAIC recommends privacy-by-design for startups regardless: [OAIC startups](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/organisations/start-ups) and [small business](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/organisations/small-business).

APP 7 applies where personal information is used for targeted online/mobile advertising. Third-party pixels also engage collection notice, use/disclosure, security and opt-out obligations: [OAIC direct marketing](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/organisations/direct-marketing) and [tracking pixels](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/organisations/tracking-pixels-and-privacy-obligations). Contextual ads that do not use personal information are the safer MVP. Publish a plain-English privacy policy, minimise analytics identifiers, document retention and deletion, and give a simple opt-out before adding profiling or adtech sharing.

The removal of the small-business exemption is not yet current law; broader second-tranche privacy reform was still under consultation in July 2026. The Children's Online Privacy Code is also still a **draft**, with final registration required by 10 December 2026: [OAIC code status](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-registers/privacy-codes/childrens-online-privacy-code). Because sports audiences include minors, design now for no personalised advertising to under-18s and no sensitive-category targeting rather than waiting for the final code.

The Spam Act applies to marketing email, SMS and instant messages, not merely because a labelled card appears inside the product. These messages require consent, accurate sender details and a no-cost unsubscribe honoured within five working days; outsourcing does not shift liability: [ACMA spam guidance](https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam).

### What is useful but unpaid unless negotiated

The official NRL watch page directs users to Nine/9Now and Foxtel/Kayo: [NRL watch](https://www.nrl.com/watch/live/live-app/). AFL's domestic 2025–2031 rights are Seven/7plus and Foxtel/Kayo: [AFL broadcast rights](https://www.afl.com.au/matches/broadcast-guide/broadcast-rights). No public first-party affiliate or per-click program was found for those broadcasters as at the checked date. An ordinary outbound CTA therefore improves the product but earns **$0** unless NothingSport signs a bespoke tracked-referral agreement.

Likewise, linking to nrl.com.au or afl.com.au is not itself a revenue arrangement. Pitch a CPA or qualified-lead pilot only after NothingSport can show click volume, audience quality and a compliant placement. Ask expressly for approved destinations, tracking, attribution window, eligible conversion, rate, clawbacks, payment threshold, reporting, brand use and privacy requirements.

### Content and brand constraints

[NRL terms](https://www.nrl.com/terms-of-use) restrict site material to personal/non-commercial use and prohibit reproduction, publication, transmission or framing without consent. NRL content services also publishes material fees, with match vision starting at $2,000, which makes imagery/video unsuitable for the lean MVP: [NRL content services](https://www.nrl.com/operations/content-services/). AFL-linked [Telstra terms](https://www.telstra.com.au/terms-of-use) permit links subject to conditions but prohibit false endorsement and unauthorised use of marks.

Therefore:

- use visible plain-text links to official watch/source pages with no suggestion NothingSport is affiliated;
- do not copy official editorial, images, video, logos, page layouts or databases;
- independently source factual fixture/result data or use it under a licence with express commercial display, cache and attribution rights;
- treat any vendor claim of “official” coverage as unverified until the contract names the competition and rights; and
- do not infer logo rights from a data or ticket API.

## 6. Commercial model and staged experiments

| Model | Upfront cash | Status | Suggested first test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Contextual, vetted native Feed ads | Low | Can sell directly now, subject to contract/compliance | Flat-price one-week sponsorship for a sport/event context; disclose and measure aggregate impressions/clicks |
| Sponsored editorial/card | Low | Possible, but higher trust risk | Only where editorial control and “Paid promotion” label are explicit |
| Ticketmaster affiliate ticket links | Low | **Confirmed program; approval required** | Apply, then show eligible event CTAs using the affiliate/API contract |
| Broadcaster watch referrals | Low technical cost | **No confirmed public payment program** | First publish useful ordinary links; later pitch a tracked CPA pilot |
| NRL/AFL site referrals | Low technical cost | **No confirmed payment program** | Keep as useful provenance; seek bespoke commercial agreement after traction |
| Official league data/widgets | Potentially material recurring cost | **Licence/quote required** | Defer until a licence materially improves acquisition or revenue |
| Premium ad-free product/features | Low rights cost | Product proposal, not externally confirmed | Test willingness to pay after retention is proven |
| B2B event-discovery widget/feed | Moderate build/sales cost | Future proposal | Pitch only after consumer feed and rights chain are stable |

For every monetised outbound link, maintain a destination/contract register and a first-party redirect such as `/out/provider/event-id`. Show the final domain, apply affiliate parameters exactly as contracted, store only necessary aggregate click data and never cloak a destination. Revenue recognition, GST and invoicing should be configured with the accountant once the first contract is known.

Wagering should not be a launch revenue line. The product is accessible to minors, gambling ads already have strict live-sport and child-targeting rules, and Parliament passed further reforms on 19 August 2026 whose commencement details require legal confirmation: [ACMA gambling rules](https://www.acma.gov.au/rules-gambling-ads) and [interactive gambling reform update](https://www.acma.gov.au/interactivegambling).

## 7. Practical 90-day sequence

### Before incorporation

- Decide the exact company name, founder initial shareholding and registered office.
- Run ASIC and trade mark searches; document NothingSport's first public use/disclosure dates.
- Inventory founder/Jim/contractor contributions and third-party code/data/assets.
- Agree a non-binding Jim term sheet covering product scope, cash pay, equity, vesting and desired governance.

### Days 1–14

- Incorporate founder-only with replaceable rules: **$636**.
- Obtain ABN/TFN; register the one-year business name if needed: **$47**.
- Open the bank account and bookkeeping file; set aside the **$342** annual ASIC fee.
- Execute founder/contributor IP assignments.
- Obtain a fixed-fee lawyer/accountant quote limited to Jim's employment/directorship, shareholder agreement, ESS/tax and IP chain of title.

### Days 15–30

- Finalise Jim's cash/working arrangement before ongoing Product Lead work.
- Approve and document the equity plan; obtain Jim's director ID and written consent.
- Either appoint Jim as **Director and Product Lead** with deadlock/conflict rules, or use Product Lead + board observer for an agreed trial period.
- Adopt privacy policy, advertiser policy, affiliate disclosure and basic terms of use.
- Apply to Ticketmaster's affiliate program and begin direct advertiser outreach.

### Days 31–90

- Run one contextual sponsorship pilot with clear labels and no behavioural tracking.
- Measure outbound watch/ticket clicks without implying payment or endorsement.
- If Ticketmaster approves the application, test attributable ticket conversions.
- File the NothingSport word mark in the minimum justified classes after clearance.
- Prepare NRL/AFL/broadcaster outreach only after there is credible user and click data.
- Seek a patent opinion only if a documented technical invention survives the disclosure/prior-art audit.

## Cash-floor summary

The unavoidable initial government cash floor is **$636**, or **$683** if a one-year NothingSport business name is also required. ABN/tax registrations and director IDs are free. Budget another **$342 every year** for ASIC review.

A lean two-class standard trade mark filing would bring the known government spend to **$1,183** ($683 formation/name + $500 trade mark). TM Headstart for two classes would make it **$1,343**. These totals exclude the most important variable costs: scoped startup legal advice, equity tax/valuation advice, proper cash remuneration for Jim, superannuation, bookkeeping and insurance.

The best place to spend scarce cash is the equity/employment/IP package—not a patent, paid sports-data licence, match imagery or programmatic-ad stack. Those can wait until NothingSport has retention, click and advertiser evidence.
