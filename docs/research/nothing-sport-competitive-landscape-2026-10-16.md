# Nothing Sport — competitive threats and market landscape

**Research date:** 16 October 2026, Australia/Sydney  
**Perspective:** Australia first; global products included where they can substitute for an Australian fan's needs or credibly expand into them.  
**Coverage:** 50 ranked primary competitors/product families, 11 additional watchlist substitutes, five technology enablers and eight structural threats.  
**Evidence:** first-party product sites, official help, developer-authored Australian app listings, company announcements and relevant primary registries. Product claims are documented rather than independently performance-tested.

## Main finding

Nothing Sport faces a crowded market, including competitors for almost every individual part of its proposition. The strongest immediate pressure comes from **Sofascore and Flashscore for the daily sports habit; Google and Apple for low-friction access; and Kayo/Foxtel, Stan/Nine and ESPN/Disney for the viewing destination**. The closest discovery/planning challenges also include ECAL World, FANZO, Fixture Calendar and several small emerging apps.

**The valuable opening is a trustworthy Australian decision service: what deserves my time, why, when and where I can watch, with control over spoilers and a firm gambling-free commitment.** That is a proposed competitive position, not proof of a protected moat or a claim that every ingredient is unique. Sources and product-specific qualifications follow in the tables and profiles.

Three findings deserve particular attention:

- **ECAL is now a consumer discovery competitor.** ECAL World adds recommendations and discovery to official-publisher calendar subscriptions. Its existing network is an execution advantage, but its claimed calendar subscribers must not be mislabelled ECAL World active users. [Official launch description](https://ecal.com/discover-ecal-world/)
- **Watchability and spoiler protection already have direct alternatives.** NoSpoilerz documents a FOMO Meter, and Thrillr and ReplayRank publish spoiler-conscious match ratings. These are credible conceptual threats despite unverified scale. [NoSpoilerz method](https://www.nospoilerz.com/fomo-meter), [Thrillr](https://www.itsathrillr.com/), [ReplayRank](https://replayrank.com/)
- **An Australian small app already closely matches the core workflow.** Score advertises follows, hidden scores, reminders, a calendar and viewing listings. Its reach is unknown, so close overlap does not automatically make it a larger immediate threat than an incumbent. [Score AU listing](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/score-your-sports-one-feed/id6782482139)

## Scope, product baseline and ranking method

The comparison treats Nothing Sport as a personalised live-sport curator and companion, with Follow, fixture/event discovery, schedules/standings, viewing routes, spoiler choices, ratings and social context. Its current approved charter excludes betting products, odds, tips, gambling advertising, bookmaker sponsorship and betting affiliate revenue.

The NS baseline was checked against `config/brand-copy.js`, `docs/follow-decisions.md`, rating/Match Centre source paths and the supplied product brief. The current local checkout differed from the moving remote-tracking reference, so the charter was checked at the fixed `origin/main` snapshot **a13f44a207d49c3a12615a1353cb5a33c648282a**. This is a source-based strategic baseline; it does not certify the current deployed build, all coverage, real community participation or physical-device delivery. Older September strategy material supplied context only; current competitor findings were refreshed.

Ranks express **relative strategic priority over the next 12–24 months**, judged from five considerations: overlap with NS's user jobs, relevance to Australian fans, ability to retain a recurring habit, distribution/execution advantages, and credible convergence towards NS's position. No market shares, measured switching probabilities or invented revenue estimates underpin these ranks. Rank differences within a tier are judgement calls, not statistically significant measurements.

| Tier | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Very high | Broad substitute or gatekeeper that can remove several reasons to use NS now. |
| High | Strong overlap or distribution; some are especially threatening within one sport or user segment. |
| Moderate | Credible partial substitute, close emerging rival, or product with meaningful local/scale uncertainty. |
| Lower / watchlist | Narrower present overlap, unverified Australian sports availability, or emerging capability worth checking. |

**Reading the evidence:** “documented” means the owner states it or the public interface exposes it. An AU App Store listing establishes storefront availability, not reliable Australian fixtures, notification delivery or streaming rights. “Not verified” is an evidence gap, not proof of absence. Rankings, limitations' business implications and suggested responses are analyst inference. Announced/coming-soon features are separated from current claims. No paid accounts, installations, full subscription audits or latency tests were performed.

Products and product families are the unit of comparison, not a count of independent companies. Kayo/Foxtel/DAZN/Fox Sports AU form one strategic entry; Nine/Stan/9Now/WWOS another. A supplier can also be a potential partner. None of the partnership possibilities below is a verified commercial offer.

## Detailed competitor profiles

Profile numbers match the ranked table. Product descriptions are first-party evidence; paragraphs labelled assessment, threat or response are strategic interpretations. No source's claim to be fastest, most accurate or largest is treated as an independently verified finding.

### 1. Sofascore

**Website:** [https://www.sofascore.com/](https://www.sofascore.com/)

**Evidence.** The [current Australian App Store developer description](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/sofascore-live-sports-scores/id1176147574) identifies 25+ sports, AFL, NRL, State of Origin, A-League, BBL, cricket Tests, tennis, F1/MotoGP and NBA. It advertises personalised goal/try/wicket/boundary alerts, following teams/leagues and a custom multi-match scoreboard. Recent release notes add a team calendar match view, an AI-curated trending grid, expanded Live Activities and paid analysis tiers. Treat these as developer claims, not measured delivery performance.

The [official TV schedule explanation](https://sofascore.helpscoutdocs.com/article/175-tv-schedule-information?lng=en) says channels combine multiple sources with community input and require sufficient confirmation; users contribute from their current country. [Official chat help](https://sofascore.helpscoutdocs.com/category/149-chat?lng=en) confirms live match chat. The [odds-hiding FAQ](https://sofascore.helpscoutdocs.com/article/165-how-to-hide-odds-information?lng=en) says the option requires an active Remove ads subscription; hiding odds also hides winning/dropping odds. [Winning-odds help](https://sofascore.helpscoutdocs.com/article/124-explanation-of-winning-odds) explicitly references its betting partner. The [corporate site](https://corporate.sofascore.com/) claims 175M+ downloads; this is cumulative self-reported downloads, not active users or Australian share.

**Assessment.** This is the most complete functional substitute among these products. Nothing Sport cannot safely position generic multi-sport following or where-to-watch as unique. The defensible angle is a deliberately curated decision about what deserves scarce viewing time, excellent Australian context, transparent uncertainty, and a consistent gambling-free experience. Sofascore's player statistical scores and player-of-match votes should not be equated with an event's entertainment or rewatch value.

**Uncertainties.** No hands-on AU channel completeness, spoiler mode or event-worth rating system verified. Help descriptions from November 2025 may lag fast-changing 2026 app releases.

### 2. Flashscore Australia

**Website:** [https://www.flashscore.com.au/](https://www.flashscore.com.au/)

**Evidence.** The [AU App Store developer listing](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/flashscore-live-scores-news/id766443283) advertises 30+ sports and 6,000+ competitions, with explicit AFL/AFLW, NRL/State of Origin, NBL/WNBL, A-League/NPL, BBL/Sheffield Shield/Ashes, golf and tennis coverage. It lists live scores, statistical player/team ratings, ladders, news, previews/recaps, favourites and alerts. [AU favourites help](https://www.flashscore.com.au/faq/capabilities/) verifies team/match following and says there is currently no calendar export. It also describes competition pinning and a 200-team/200-match limit.

[AU odds help](https://www.flashscore.com.au/faq/odds/) states that Flashscore compares bookmaker odds, does not itself accept bets, shows odds/ads from partner bookmakers, and currently cannot remove odds. [AU subscription help](https://www.flashscore.com.au/faq/ads/) says iOS subscriptions remove banners but retain betting content and some sponsorship/third-party-player ads. Newer [official news-notification instructions](https://www.flashscore.com/news/transfers-reactions-interviews-turn-on-notifications-for-news-about-your-favourite-players-and-teams/6ss0xaph/) describe followed football players and teams, individually configurable news alerts and confirmed-transfer alerts.

**Assessment.** A very strong default for Australian breadth, schedules and the real-time match journey. Its editorial volume competes with attention as well as utility. Nothing Sport should not attempt a raw coverage-count contest; clean curation and choice support are a better distinction. A banner-free subscription is not equivalent to Nothing Sport's gambling-free charter.

**Source conflict.** AU FAQ still says player following is not yet available, while 2026 app notes and official news help describe it as shipped. Prefer newer feature-specific evidence and do not claim “no player following.” No full AU streaming guide, spoiler mode or entertainment rating system verified.

### 3. Google Search / Google app

**Website:** [https://www.google.com/](https://www.google.com/)

Google's June 2026 soccer product announcement documents scores, lineups, standings, brackets and following a match/team for Lock Screen updates on iOS and Android. Its Android support page specifies device/version/manufacturer limitations. These are existing sports functions, not a hypothetical future launch. [Soccer announcement](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/soccer-tournament-google-tools-2026/), [Android support](https://support.google.com/android/answer/17117407?hl=en)

Its September NFL Live Game Feed and fantasy integrations were announced for US-English mobile at launch. They demonstrate direction, not verified Australian availability. The broader May Search announcement also describes information agents using fresh sports information, with rollout limitations; no all-sport Australian autonomous assistant was tested. [NFL announcement](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/football-features-google-search/), [Search direction](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/search-io-2026/)

**Threat assessment:** Google can intercept a fan's question before any specialist app opens. The immediate risk is fewer reasons to develop a separate NS habit; the potential risk is increasingly personalised answers and briefings. NS needs a saved, trustworthy decision experience that is materially easier than repeating searches. Its opportunity is consistent preferences, transparent source freshness, neutral local viewing choices and deliberate spoiler control. ## What is differentiated, what is vulnerable

| NS proposition | Competitive finding | Strength of the potential defence |
|---|---|---|
| Multi-sport follows, fixtures, scores and reminders | Widely documented across incumbents and emerging planners | Low by itself; execution and reliability matter |
| Australian sporting context | Strong local alternatives include Flashscore, Sofascore, official apps and broadcasters | Useful focus; not exclusive |
| Neutral decision across subscription services | Broadcasters favour their own inventory; independent guides still compete directly | Promising if local rights and practical routing are demonstrably better |
| Spoiler-safe browsing | NoSpoilerz, TNNS, F1, Live Soccer TV and Score document relevant controls | Valuable user benefit; not unique; full-journey quality can distinguish |
| What deserves my time / event quality | FOMO Meter, Thrillr, ReplayRank and excitement-data suppliers overlap | Potentially strong with trusted judgement and real outcomes; idea alone weak |
| Real-user/friend ratings and social context | Match chat/community exists elsewhere; NS's precise rating/social combination was not matched here | Potential network value; unproven until real groups participate |
| Gambling-free features and funding | Several rivals explicitly include odds, bookmaker links or media/betting convergence; some offer hiding | Clear values-led distinction; avoid claiming NS is the only such service |

These are proposed defences and risks, not validated market demand. The sources in the gambling/feature comparison and detailed profiles support the factual overlap; the judgement about defensibility is inference.

## Recommended priorities

1. **Benchmark the complete decision journey against ten products first:** Sofascore, Flashscore, Apple Sports, Kayo, Stan, FANZO, ECAL World, Fixture Calendar, Score and NoSpoilerz. Add FotMob or CA Live for the chosen first sport cohort. Use the same real fixtures and subscriptions. Measure correct local time, participant inclusion, reschedules, reminder arrival, spoiler exposure and the actual viewing destination. Desk-research checkmarks are insufficient.
2. **Choose the user before broadening the feature set.** A time-poor Australian multi-sport viewer with several subscriptions is a more coherent starting hypothesis than every sports fan. Test it against football-only, cricket-only and replay-first cohorts; those cohorts face different rivals.
3. **Make the distinct value visible in one screen.** A useful candidate is a short followed-sport decision list with a clear reason, correct time, trustworthy watch route and spoiler choice. More rows and stats will not by themselves defeat incumbents.
4. **Prove that Nothing Score helps a decision.** Compare a user's own and friends' ratings with editorial and algorithmic suggestions. Show real sample sizes and uncertainty. Do not let a rating override explicit follow rules. Check that spoiler-safe labels avoid revealing decisive events through commentary or thumbnails.
5. **Treat gambling independence as an enduring promise, not a discovery claim.** Publish the scope clearly and test whether it drives preference or payment. Other apps can hide odds, and some may independently share similar values; this report does not prove exclusivity.
6. **Maintain a bounded competitor review.** Recheck Apple/Google local-code and feature expansion, ECAL World, Score/NoSpoilerz product development and broadcaster consolidation when planning material product changes. This recommendation does not create an automation or authorise implementation.

Useful outcome measures are repeated unaided opening, fewer external cross-checks, a completed path to the right viewing service, accurate reminder delivery, spoiler incidents and willingness to pay. The practical competitive test is whether users prefer NS for a real weekly job, not whether a matrix contains more ticks.

## Evidence limitations and research record

All sources were accessed during this research dated 16 October 2026. Historical announcements retain their dates. An undated current page is a snapshot at access; it does not establish a release date. The public sources cited here are primary for what each owner offers or claims, not independent audits of quality or market traction. A publisher's viewing article is primary evidence of its competitive format, not final authority over the underlying broadcast rights.

No current Australian active-user, retention, switching, revenue or acquisition-cost dataset was verified. Download totals, ratings counts, page traffic and cumulative calendar subscribers were not conflated. The rankings therefore deserve more confidence at the category/tier level than at adjacent numerical positions. Genuine gaps are stated, rather than scored as absent.

Source access was uneven: FANZO/selected publisher pages were blocked or cookie-gated, ECAL World required JavaScript, and some app-store caches lagged the AU listing. First-party indexed content and developer descriptions were used where appropriate. Score's live website was independently retrieved over HTTPS after the research browser failed. No blocked page was treated as evidence that a product had shut down.

The full ranked set is a comprehensive, bounded landscape for NS's strategic question; it is not a guarantee that every niche sports app worldwide is included. The lists favour credible substitutes with a clear mechanism and primary evidence. Deep profiles include their evidence links and unknowns, so a future researcher can revisit the important claims.
