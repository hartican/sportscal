## User outcome and evidence

Verified history can arrive later, preserving Results privacy and existing source links/focus. A closed or superseded profile cannot be reopened or overwritten by its late response. This is a candidate awaiting final release proof below.

The standings control was usable earlier (1,836 ms and 50 ms respectively). The second genuine production history request took 1,307 ms, returned 200, non-stale and no history. The first has no completed response receipt; its recorded duration must not be presented as a successful request or a precise server-latency measurement. First-open timings also include directory/module loading and interaction, which this repair does not remove. These two observations are evidence of a specific journey, not a population performance benchmark.

The browser used synthetic local Football preferences, isolated other backend APIs and blocked service workers; the history requests were genuine production reads. No authenticated account, physical phone or invited cohort is implied. The earlier large-download premise was rejected: the static athlete-history dataset is about 6 KB. This repair concerns a demonstrated critical-path wait, not removal of a large dataset.

The tight red command was `BROWSER_ENGINE=chromium node scripts/validate-profile-progressive-browser.js`, with the existing local Playwright module configured. It drives the public profile renderer and holds its optional history response. The known club heading remains absent and the 250-ms assertion fails. This isolates the blocking dependency without relying on variable public network timing. The red log is retained under the `profile-progressive` prefix in the existing delivery folder.
