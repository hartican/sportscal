# Official fixture highlights

Approved 13–14 October 2026 (Australia/Sydney). Tennis is the first ingestion adapter; fixture metadata and the Watch highlights action are sport-neutral.

## Product rules

- Completed senior men's/women's singles and senior men's/women's/mixed doubles qualify from the sourced last 16 onward. Earlier rounds, qualifying, junior/wheelchair draws, team ties and unknown stages do not qualify automatically. A round number needs a sourced tournament round total; “round four” alone is not the policy.
- Only reviewed official rights-holder channel IDs qualify. The initial registry covers ATP Tour, WTA, Tennis TV and the four Grand Slams. New channels need a reviewed official identity and competition scope.
- Use standalone match highlights with an exact edition/tournament, draw and participants binding. All four doubles players must match. Exclude Shorts, interviews, point clips and compilations. Titles/thumbnails never appear on the card.
- The YouTube mark and Watch highlights label are visible on fully minimised, summary and expanded cards, including Results OFF. The canonical external YouTube URL opens normally. Pending/unknown videos have no action.
- Media does not create fixtures, grant Feed eligibility, change retention, alter sporting clocks, rate a fixture, enable alerts or change follows.

## Refresh ownership and fallbacks

`node scripts/update-cards.js` remains the only canonical owner. Full and quick refreshes include media discovery/projection. `node scripts/update-cards.js --highlights --local-only` is the scoped source/projection path. The existing protected live refresh has one `discovery-tennis-highlights` source, due every 30 minutes, behind current score work, with the existing leases/deadline/backoff. The media owner uses the existing two-read participant bundle for the complete Tennis sport family, including new roster/source arrivals, unknown rows and required published/archive aliases. It avoids a whole-world fixture projection while preserving the three-second database deadlines; it adds no RPC, query or scheduler. No new scheduler or browser polling loop exists.

Official public Atom feeds are the default. An approved feed supplies the video ID, publisher, publication date and normal-watch/Shorts link. Public oEmbed confirms video/publisher availability. These feeds expose a rolling recent-upload window; cached candidates retain 256 videos. This is not an exhaustive historical index. Seven-day cards are attempted first, then bounded archived references. Optional `YOUTUBE_DATA_API_KEY` enables channel-upload fallback, batched video/region verification and at most two historical searches per due pass. API-only videos of three minutes or less remain pending when ordinary-video versus Shorts classification is unknown.

Public feeds/oEmbed do not expose territory restrictions. Their record uses `regionStatus: unknown`; no Australian playback guarantee is manufactured. API-confirmed Australian restrictions suppress the link and survive loss of the key. Deleted/private links are cleared after authoritative failure; transient failures retain the prior verified link. Existing links are rechecked daily. Metadata and availability expire after 30 days unless their provider checks succeed. `verifiedAt` keeps first provenance, `checkedAt` records a successful availability check, and `sourceCheckedAt` records a successful full metadata read. Check-only timestamps are already excluded from sporting revisions and card fingerprints. oEmbed alone cannot renew an old publication/description receipt; failed attempts and overall coverage timestamps never renew either bound. The existing owner emits explicit withdrawals for expired retained records, removes old candidate metadata and negative availability, and purges the canonical cache even in offline/failure projection. Readers hide expired cached actions. Unchanged sporting hashes leave stored fixture rows alone; the existing two-read transport projects renewed receipts only from matching, successful per-video checks in the owner’s saved private health document. A separate transport validator delivers those renewed dates to cached clients without a sporting revision or card-state change. Stale transport caches also withdraw expired media when database reads fail. No new database request, job or viewer-triggered write is added.

The sport-neutral `fixture-highlights.v1` record contains provider, video ID, exact canonical URL, approved channel ID, publisher publication date, verification date, public availability, region status, standalone flag and matching evidence. Video titles are retained for matching but never rendered. Optional `fixture.highlights: null` withdraws media explicitly. Backend source enrichment changes this field only and cannot create a missing fixture.

## Archives

The archive owner preserves source tournament/draw/round/participant metadata and highlights. The media source uses the archive owner's service-only `nothingsports_referenced_classics` and `nothingsports_hydrate_classic_highlights` RPCs. It pages 200 shared fixture references at a time, without account identity, and writes only changed media. A missing/not-yet-deployed archive schema is reported as pending and does not break recent discovery. Archive readers use stored media, with a read-only static-cache fallback for snapshots lacking the field. A stored record or explicit withdrawal always wins; viewer reads perform no media-provider request or enrichment write. Frozen editorial and ordinary Feed retirement remain archive-owned.

## Cost and activation

The public-feed path needs no API key. Expected YouTube API charge at this volume is A$0/month; even an API-first seven-channel/30-minute design is roughly 400–500 ordinary quota units daily, before historical searches. The optional historical search budget is up to 96 calls/day in Google's separate 100-call daily search bucket; each search costs one unit in that bucket. Other endpoints share the default 10,000 ordinary units per day. Only the shared thirty-minute live owner runs historical searches; canonical full/quick/scoped projections set their search budget to zero so they cannot duplicate that allowance. Premium is a viewing subscription and does not supply an API key or extra API quota. Hosting/database usage remains within the existing owners and is separate from YouTube API quota.

Sources checked 14 October 2026: [Google quota calculator](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/determine_quota_cost), [quota extensions](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/guides/quota_and_compliance_audits), [Premium benefits](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10159588?hl=en). Adding a server-only key later requires a deployment with that environment; no key is shipped to browsers or committed.

## Verification

`validate-fixture-highlights.js` exercises real source contracts, matching/round boundaries, outage/removal/regional handling, store cadence, archive RPC boundaries, schema and canonical full/quick owners. `validate-fixture-highlights-browser.js` exercises the actual shared Feed/Events card renderer in Chromium/WebKit, three widths, both themes, repeated expansion, Results OFF, destination/touch sizing and actual failed-image fallback. Release also requires existing startup/performance, Follow, live fixture and installed/offline checks. Browser evidence cannot certify physical iPhone behaviour. `validate-fixture-highlights-cache.js` uses controlled replay clocks and synthetic archived dates to test the exact 30-day boundary, failed/partial checks, renewed classics and offline cleanup; these captures do not prove current availability or historical sports coverage. Legacy cache entries without full metadata receipts expire conservatively from their first verification or publication date. Policy: [YouTube Developer Policies III.E.4](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/developer-policies), checked 14 October 2026.

## Played-match start display

The current repair preserves the publisher-reported post-match start separately as actualStartTimeUtc/actualStartEvidence. The evidence records publisher-post-match-startDate explicitly. The card says REPORTED START, rounded to the nearest five minutes, while the original UTC value and scheduled/reminder clock remain intact. This is not independent certification of first serve. A played fixture without supported timing says START TBD and does not reuse an old Not before bound.

The production no-key path reads the public feeds and metadata endpoints and opens normal YouTube links. It does not download media, scrape player internals, bypass access restrictions or establish Australian playback where region metadata is unavailable.
