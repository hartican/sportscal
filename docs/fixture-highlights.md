# Fixture highlights

Approved 13–15 October 2026 (Australia/Sydney). Tennis is the first ingestion adapter; fixture metadata and the Watch highlights action are sport-neutral.

## Product rules

- Completed senior men's/women's singles and senior men's/women's/mixed doubles qualify at every round, including qualifying and round-robin individual matches. Junior/wheelchair draws, team ties and tournament overviews remain outside this individual-match adapter. Seven-day Feed cards qualify without changing existing follows or admission. A sourced round total is required to convert an ordinal round to a draw size; no universal Round 4 = Last 16 assumption.
- Verified official media requires reviewed rights-holder channel IDs; the separately approved automatic top search result may use any publisher. The initial registry covers ATP Tour, WTA, Tennis TV and the four Grand Slams. New channels need a reviewed official identity and competition scope.
- Use standalone match highlights with an exact edition/tournament, draw and participants binding. All four doubles players must match. Exclude Shorts, interviews, point clips and compilations. Titles/thumbnails never appear on the card.
- The YouTube mark and Watch highlights label are visible on fully minimised, summary and expanded cards, including Results OFF. A valid official record opens its canonical video directly. For completed named fixtures already in the seven-day Feed, the later 15 October approval opens YouTube’s own first relevance-search video automatically through the existing selected fixture route. The tooltip explicitly says the match is unverified. Identity, competition, sport, date/session and Tennis player/tournament/year queries retain their existing owner. Search selections grant no official-match or playback guarantee and never enter the verified media field. There is no results chooser. Verified links retain their own expiry/restriction rules; the recent fallback may select a different upload.
- Media does not create fixtures, grant Feed eligibility, change retention, alter sporting clocks, rate a fixture, enable alerts or change follows.

Official public Atom feeds are the default. An approved feed supplies the video ID, publisher, publication date and normal-watch/Shorts link. Public oEmbed confirms video/publisher availability. Optional `YOUTUBE_DATA_API_KEY` enables channel-upload fallback, batched video/region verification and at most two historical searches per due pass. Public feeds/oEmbed do not expose territory restrictions. Their record uses `regionStatus: unknown`; no Australian playback guarantee is manufactured. API-confirmed Australian restrictions suppress the link and survive loss of the key. Deleted/private links are cleared after authoritative failure; transient failures retain the prior verified link. Existing links are rechecked daily. Metadata and availability expire after 30 days unless their provider checks succeed. `verifiedAt` keeps first provenance, `checkedAt` records a successful availability check, and `sourceCheckedAt` records a successful full metadata read. Check-only timestamps are already excluded from sporting revisions and card fingerprints. oEmbed alone cannot renew an old publication/description receipt; failed attempts and overall coverage timestamps never renew either bound. Unchanged sporting hashes leave stored fixture rows alone; the existing two-read transport projects renewed receipts only from matching, successful per-video checks in the owner’s saved private health document. The sport-neutral `fixture-highlights.v1` record contains provider, video ID, exact canonical URL, approved channel ID, publisher publication date, verification date, public availability, region status, standalone flag and matching evidence. Video titles are retained for matching but never rendered. Optional `fixture.highlights: null` withdraws media explicitly. Backend source enrichment changes this field only and cannot create a missing fixture.

Premium is a viewing subscription and does not supply an API key or extra API quota. Hosting/database usage remains within the existing owners and is separate from YouTube API quota.
